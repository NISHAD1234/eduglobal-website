Why Study MBBS in Uzbekistan

Uzbekistan has become one of the fastest-growing destinations for Indian students who want an affordable, NMC-compliant medical degree abroad. With low tuition fees, a climate similar to North India, direct flights from Delhi, and government-run medical universities, it has emerged as a strong alternative to Russia, Georgia, Kyrgyzstan, and Kazakhstan.

  • Low tuition fees: Annual tuition at reputed government universities ranges roughly from USD 2,400–3,500 (about ₹2–3 lakh per year), making the full course significantly cheaper than private medical colleges in India
  • Similar climate: Cities such as Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara, and Fergana have a continental climate — hot summers and cold (but not extreme) winters — that Indian students generally adapt to quickly
  • Short flight distance: Delhi to Tashkent is a direct flight of roughly 3 hours, keeping travel costs and time low
  • Affordable cost of living: Everyday expenses (food, local transport, groceries) are inexpensive, and the Indian Rupee is stronger than the Uzbek Som (roughly ₹1 ≈ 150 Som, though exchange rates fluctuate)
  • English-medium teaching: Instruction is in English throughout the course at NMC-compliant universities, with many colleges also employing Indian faculty for select subjects
  • Hostel and Indian food availability: University hostels are the norm for international students, and Indian mess facilities or nearby Indian restaurants are available in major student cities
  • Government universities: Most recommended institutions are state-run, which generally means more stable fee structures and recognized accreditation

Uzbekistan opened its higher education system to international medical students in significant numbers from around 2018 onward and has since become a popular hub for Indian MBBS aspirants, alongside Russia, Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, and Georgia.

MBBS Course Duration & Structure

  • Total duration: 6 years — 5 years of academic study plus 1 year of mandatory clinical internship, typically completed at university-affiliated hospitals in Uzbekistan
  • Medium of instruction: English, for the full duration of the course at NMC-compliant universities
  • Local language: Learning Uzbek (and/or Russian) is usually compulsory, mainly to support patient interaction during clinical postings and daily life
  • Recognition: Many Uzbek medical universities are listed with the World Directory of Medical Schools (WDOMS)

ℹ️ After completing MBBS in Uzbekistan and returning to India, students must qualify the FMGE (Foreign Medical Graduate Examination) — or its successor exam once implemented — and complete a further supervised internship in India, as required under NMC rules, before they can practice.

Eligibility Criteria

To be eligible for MBBS admission in Uzbekistan, Indian students typically need:

  • Age: At least 17 years old by December 31 of the admission year
  • Academic qualification: Passed 10+2 with Physics, Chemistry, and Biology, with a minimum of 50% aggregate marks (relaxations may apply for reserved categories, depending on the university)
  • NEET-UG: A qualifying NEET score in the current year or the previous two years (mandatory for any student who wants to practice medicine in India after graduating)
  • Valid passport

ℹ️ Entry requirements at Uzbek medical universities are generally less stringent than in the USA, UK, Australia, or the EU, which is one reason the country has become popular for MBBS abroad.

Is Uzbekistan Good for MBBS?

Uzbekistan is worth considering for students who prioritize affordability, a climate reasonably close to India's, and a manageable travel distance. Universities with a longer track record of hosting Indian students, established clinical training infrastructure, and available Indian faculty tend to offer a smoother transition. As with any study-abroad decision, prospective students should compare specific universities on facilities, faculty strength, hostel quality, and past FMGE outcomes rather than relying on country-level reputation alone.

NMC FMGL Regulations — Is MBBS from Uzbekistan Valid in India?

Indian graduates of foreign medical universities must meet the National Medical Commission's Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate (FMGL) Regulations, 2021 to practice in India. Key requirements include:

NMC RequirementHow MBBS in Uzbekistan Generally Complies
Minimum 54 months of academic instructionStandard Uzbek MBBS programs run 5 years of academics (60 months), satisfying this
12-month internshipCompleted in the 6th year at the university's affiliated teaching hospital in Uzbekistan
English medium of instruction throughoutCompliant universities teach entirely in English; verify this individually before enrolling, as not every institution or program guarantees it
Registration with the host country's regulatory bodyUzbekistan does not require a separate licensing exam for foreign graduates; registration is typically automatic upon graduation
Completion within 10 years of joiningStudents must finish the full FMG course (including internship) within 10 years of starting

After returning to India, graduates must clear the FMGE (or its replacement exam once notified by NMC) and complete a further Compulsory Rotating Medical Internship (CRMI) in India before receiving a full license to practice.

⚠️ This is general regulatory information and not legal advice. Always verify the latest FMGL Regulations directly on the NMC website (nmc.org.in) and confirm a specific university's compliance before taking admission.

FMGE Passing Percentage

Since Uzbekistan is a relatively newer MBBS destination for Indian students, historical FMGE data is still limited compared to countries like Russia or Ukraine. Published results for recent years show wide variation by university — some institutions have recorded pass rates in the 30–48% range, while others are considerably lower, particularly for newer batches with smaller sample sizes.

ℹ️ Because these figures shift from year to year and by cohort size, request the latest official pass-rate data for the exact universities you're considering rather than relying on a single year's numbers.

Cost of Living & Hostel Facilities

  • Monthly living cost: Food, local transport, and daily essentials are generally affordable; many students report manageable monthly expenses well below what similar living would cost in Western Europe or the US
  • Hostel accommodation: Provided by nearly all universities for international students, usually in 2–4 seater rooms, furnished, with utilities (electricity, water) typically included in the fee
  • Indian food: Indian mess facilities are available at many universities, and cities like Tashkent and Samarkand have a good number of Indian restaurants
  • Currency exchange: The Indian Rupee is generally stronger than the Uzbek Som, which helps keep day-to-day costs low for Indian students, though exact conversion rates fluctuate and should be checked at the time of travel

Intake Dates & Step-by-Step Admission Process

Most Uzbek medical universities admit international students twice a year:

  • September intake — main intake, most popular
  • March intake — smaller, limited seats at some universities

ℹ️ Applying soon after NEET results are declared is generally recommended, since it allows enough time to process the admission letter and visa before the semester begins.

Step-by-Step Admission Process

  • 1Shortlist universities based on budget, location, and facilities
  • 2Submit scanned copies of academic documents, passport, and NEET scorecard for the invitation/admission letter
  • 3Receive the admission letter from the university
  • 4Submit original documents for visa processing (through the university's international office or an admission consultant)
  • 5Obtain the student visa for Uzbekistan
  • 6Arrange travel and hostel check-in before the semester start date
  • 7Complete local registration/documentation formalities after arrival, as required by the university and local authorities

Frequently Asked Questions

Is NEET compulsory for MBBS in Uzbekistan?
Yes, if you intend to practice medicine in India after graduating. NEET qualification is mandatory to appear for the FMGE.
Are there scholarships for MBBS in Uzbekistan?
Scholarships for Indian students are limited to non-existent at most universities; the majority of students self-fund or use an education loan.
Is the medium of instruction really English throughout?
At NMC-compliant universities, yes — but this should be verified for each specific university and program before applying, since practices can vary.
Do I need to learn Uzbek or Russian?
Most universities require it, primarily to support communication with patients during clinical training and daily interactions with locals.
How much does the full 6-year MBBS in Uzbekistan cost?
Tuition alone across 6 years typically ranges from roughly ₹17–24 lakh depending on the university, excluding hostel, food, travel, visa, and other charges — always request a complete, itemized cost breakdown from our counsellors.
