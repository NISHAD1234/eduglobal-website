🇰🇿 MBBS in Kazakhstan

Study MBBS in Kazakhstan

One of the most popular MBBS-abroad destinations for Indian students — English-medium teaching, mostly-public universities, budget-friendly fees, and a straightforward admission process.

Overview

Kazakhstan is one of the most popular destinations for Indian students pursuing MBBS abroad. As of 2026, the program officially runs 5+1 years — 5 years of coursework followed by a 1-year internship in Kazakhstan — with total course credits now at 490 (up from 360 previously). Annual tuition typically ranges from USD 3,800 to USD 6,500 depending on the university.

More than 10,000 Indian students are currently studying MBBS in Kazakhstan, placing it among the top 5 countries for Indian medical students abroad. Most universities are public, the entire course is taught in English (alongside compulsory local-language classes), and — unlike Russia or China — the licensing exam is also available in English. Kazakhstan generally works out cheaper than Egypt, Nepal, Mauritius, or EU options like Romania and Bulgaria, and its admission/visa process is considered comparatively simple.

Eligibility for Indian Students

  • Must clear an English proficiency exam (IELTS, Duolingo, TOEFL, etc.)
  • Must be at least 17 years old on or before December 31 of the admission year
  • Must have completed 12th grade from a recognised board
  • General category: minimum 50% aggregate in Physics, Chemistry, Biology, with English as a required subject
  • SC/ST/OBC category: minimum 40% aggregate in Physics, Chemistry, Biology, with English as a required subject
  • Must have qualified NEET (current year or previous 2 attempts are valid for MBBS abroad)

FMGL / NMC Regulation Compliance

Per the Embassy of India in Astana, Kazakhstan's MBBS program (5 years study + 1 year internship) complies with the NMC's FMGL Regulations (notified November 2021). After graduating, Indian students can appear for Kazakhstan's licensing exam — conducted by the National Centre of Independent Testing — in Kazakh, Russian, or English. This is a notable advantage over Russia or China, where licensing exams are only offered in the local language.

MBBS in Kazakhstan Without NEET

⚠️ Indian students must qualify NEET (valid within the current or previous 2 years) to be eligible for FMGE/NExT and to practice medicine in India afterward. Students who don't intend to practice in India can technically study MBBS in Kazakhstan without NEET, but they would then be ineligible for FMGE/NExT and Indian licensure.

Course Duration, Path After Graduation & Medium of Instruction

  • Complete 5 years of coursework + a 1-year internship in Kazakhstan (6 years total)
  • Clear Kazakhstan's licensing exam (takes a few additional months)
  • Return to India and appear for NExT (screening exam)
  • If NExT is cleared, complete a 1-year internship in India

Medium of Instruction

  • All lessons are delivered in English
  • The local language (Kazakh/Russian) is also taught, starting from the 1st semester, at no additional fee
  • Local language is needed for patient communication during clinical years and for daily life (shopping, cabs, etc.)

When to Apply & Admission Process

  • Students who qualified NEET in the previous 3 years can apply in April–May
  • First-time NEET candidates can apply right after writing NEET if confident of qualifying
  • Classes begin in September; some universities allow arrival in October
  • A February–March intake is also available at certain universities
  • Admission is first-come-first-served with limited seats — early application is recommended

The September-intake process typically starts around May/June; the February/March-intake process starts accepting applications around January.

Fees, Scholarships & Education Loans

  • MBBS fees in Kazakhstan generally range from USD 3,800 to USD 6,500 per year, with an estimated total budget of ₹35–55 lakh depending on the university
  • No scholarships are generally available for MBBS in Kazakhstan
  • Students typically self-fund or take an education loan from an Indian bank
  • Tuition can usually be paid annually or semester-wise — not necessarily in one lump sum
  • Hostel fees are typically annual/semi-annual; mess charges are usually monthly

Checklist: What to Verify Before Choosing a University

  • Confirm the university satisfies NMC's FMGL Regulations (required for the Indian licensing exam)
  • Check the university's age and when it started offering MBBS — avoid very newly launched MBBS programs
  • Check how many Indian students have already graduated from the university
  • Check the FMGE passing percentage — prefer universities with a higher passing rate
  • Don't rely on general World Ranking as a deciding factor — it can be misleading for this purpose
  • Confirm the fee structure fits your budget, and compare it against similar-priced alternatives
  • Check the average monthly temperature of the city you'll be living in for ~6 years

Advantages & Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Indian instructors at several (not all) universities
  • 6-year total duration including internship
  • Reasonable FMGE passing percentage at top-performing universities
  • Affordable tuition compared to most other countries
  • Affordable living expenses, including budget-friendly hostels
  • Generally considered a safe country for international students
  • Simple visa process
  • WHO-recognised degree — eligible to sit USMLE and PLAB exams
  • Indian mess facilities at almost all universities
  • Hostel facilities widely available
  • Indian Embassy present in Astana for consular support

Disadvantages

  • Sub-zero temperatures for ~3 months a year (though buildings are centrally heated, ~25°C indoors)
  • Low FMGE passing percentage at many universities — varies widely by college
  • Newer MBBS programs at several universities despite the institutions themselves being old
  • Pricing comparable to other study-abroad destinations — better options may exist at a similar budget
  • Need to learn a new language (Kazakh/Russian) alongside medical studies
  • Mandatory licensing exam in Kazakhstan before practicing (unlike, e.g., Timor-Leste, which grants a direct license)

FMGE Passing Percentage — Year by Year

In the 2021 aggregate attempt, 2,523 Indian graduates of Kazakhstan universities appeared for FMGE, with a passing percentage roughly between 24% and 30% across universities like Kazakh National Medical University, Astana Medical University, South Kazakh Medical Academy, and Semey State Medical University.

ℹ️ If a candidate appeared in both the June and December sessions of a year, NBE counts them as two separate entries, which can understate the true per-candidate passing rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which cities have milder weather?
Southern cities like Almaty and Shymkent tend to be milder than the north/central regions (Astana, Karaganda, Pavlodar) — we can point you toward specific universities based on climate preference.
What's the total 6-year budget?
Roughly ₹22–37 lakh in tuition alone across the 6-year course, plus hostel, food, flights, insurance and visa costs — ask our counselors for a full breakdown for your shortlisted universities.
Is a local entrance exam required?
Requirements vary by university — we'll confirm the exact process for your chosen college.
Can I study MBBS in Kazakhstan without NEET?
You can technically enroll, but without a qualifying NEET score (valid for the current or previous 2 years) you won't be eligible for FMGE/NExT or licensure to practice in India.
